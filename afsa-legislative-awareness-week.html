How the week works

Three moves every chapter can make

Building a strong chapter legislative program starts with engaging local elected officials. When Members are home for a district or state work period, they want to hear from the people who live and serve in their district — and AFSA can provide the stories and solutions that drive meaningful change.

Move 2, continued

Host & Recognize

Hosting a Member at a chapter meeting — or asking an official to issue a proclamation — raises community awareness, strengthens relationships with public officials, and highlights AFSA's advocacy for Airmen, Guardians, veterans, retirees, and their families.

How to request a proclamation

Step 1
Submit the request now

Offices need lead time to prepare a proclamation — the sooner your request is in, the easier it is for a busy office to say yes.

Step 2
Contact the local office

Reach the district, constituent services, or scheduling office — not Washington, D.C., when applicable.

Step 3
Send the full package

Include a draft proclamation, event details, and an AFSA point of contact so staff have everything in one place.

Step 4
Follow up within a week

No response? A polite phone call one week later keeps your request moving.

The request package

  • Cover email or letter
  • Draft proclamation
  • One-page event summary
  • AFSA organizational overview
  • Invitation to attend and present the proclamation

Ground rule

Advocate as a Private Citizen — Not in Uniform

AFSA chapter legislative engagement is conducted in your personal capacity: out of uniform, on personal time, using personal email. That keeps members consistent with Department of War (DoW) ethics guidance and Department of the Air Force policy, and avoids any appearance of official DoW endorsement of AFSA priorities. This is best-practice guidance, not legal advice — consult your Staff Judge Advocate or Ethics Counselor when questions arise.

Before every meeting, call, or event

Out of uniform. Personal capacity. Personal email. Introduce yourself as a constituent and AFSA member — not as a representative of your unit, the Air Force, or the DoW. Do not use your government email or duty title in any outreach.

As a private citizen & AFSA member
Personal capacity, out of uniform — consistent with DoW guidance
  • Meet with Members of Congress and staff, or host them at a chapter event
  • Educate the office on quality-of-life issues and share personal experiences that illustrate impacts to readiness
  • Explain what pending legislation would do and support AFSA legislative priorities
  • Participate in grassroots campaigns and write personal letters to elected officials
  • Build professional relationships through education and dialogue
Keep uniform and official duty out of it
Activities to avoid
  • Conducting AFSA legislative outreach in uniform or in an official capacity
  • Requesting that Members of Congress support or oppose legislation while acting officially or in uniform
  • Encouraging attendees to contact Congress, circulating petitions, or using advocacy QR codes or pre-written letters during official activities
  • Participating in partisan political activities while representing the Department of the Air Force or the DoW
  • Creating the appearance of official DoW endorsement of AFSA legislative priorities

If the topic comes up while you are on duty

Keep it to education and awareness: explain the issue factually, share your experience, and point people to AFSA. Save the ask — supporting a bill, contacting Congress — for when you are acting as a private citizen. In uniform, inform. Out of uniform, advocate.

Major focus groups in the 2026–2027 platform
  • Support full military pay raises in line with current law.
  • Protect and strengthen BAH, COLA, special pays, leave policies, commissaries, exchanges, and MWR programs.
  • Improve Regular Military Compensation to reflect real-world economic conditions.
  • Expand bereavement leave and allow voluntary leave transfer between service members.
  • Strengthen oversight of privatized military housing and enforce the tenant bill of rights.
  • Improve access to VA healthcare, reduce claims backlogs, and fully fund ancillary support programs.
  • Extend concurrent receipt to all disabled retirees, ending the offset of earned benefits.
  • Support full implementation of the PACT Act to ensure veterans exposed to toxic substances receive timely and complete benefits.
  • Expand access to Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy (HBOT) for treatment of persistent TBI and PTSD/PTSI.
  • Strengthen suicide prevention efforts through research, treatment access, and caregiver support.
  • Ensure women veterans receive equitable access to high-quality, gender-specific care.
  • Ensure veterans have access to emerging and alternative therapies, including psychedelic-assisted treatments, under strict medical supervision, with full informed consent, safety protocols, and evidence-based evaluation.
  • Guarantee veterans' right to informed consent across all treatment options, with clear disclosure of risks, benefits, alternatives, and the voluntary nature of participation in any medical or therapeutic intervention.
  • Preserve earned TRICARE benefits and oppose fee increases and higher pharmacy copays.
  • Expand access to healthcare benefits to not fall behind private sector and other federal employees.
  • Ensure any military medical billet reductions or MTF restructuring do not reduce access or shift costs onto families.
  • Allow pre-tax payment of health, dental, and long-term care premiums to reduce out-of-pocket burden.
  • Expand access to TRICARE Reserve Select, including for Air Reserve Technicians.
  • Expand access to affordable, high-quality childcare aligned with operational realities.
  • Strengthen military spouse employment programs and eliminate PCS-driven licensure barriers.
  • Promote policies that accommodate military lifestyles, including tax and payment flexibility.
  • Preserve Impact Aid to ensure military-connected children receive consistent educational support.
  • Fully fund MilTA, MyCAA, and GI Bill programs and preserve benefit transferability.
  • Ensure all components of the Total Force have equitable access to federal tuition assistance.
  • Improve credit transfer and prior learning recognition to reduce time-to-degree and cost.
  • Focus on empowering the Airman for future success before, during and after transition.
  • Allow surviving spouses to receive the full final month of retired pay.
  • Permit DIC recipients to remarry at age 55 without loss of benefits.
  • Protect survivor benefits from offsets or erosion under budgetary pressures.
  • Credit all post-9/11 active-duty service toward Guard and Reserve early retirement.
  • Reduce the retirement age and remove caps on creditable inactive duty points.
  • Expand education benefits for Guard members and dual-status technicians.
  • Reform duty status authorities to ensure compensation, benefits, and protections match service performed.

Active advocacy — before Congress now

GI Bill Transferability Reform

AFSA is advocating for GI Bill transferability reform — updating the transfer rules so service members can share their earned Post-9/11 GI Bill benefits with the families they serve for. A benefit earned in uniform shouldn't expire at the front gate.

The problem

Under current law, GI Bill benefits can only be transferred while still serving — with a four-year additional service obligation. Members who separate or retire before transferring, or who welcome children later in life, lose the option, no matter how many years they served.

The fix

Let the transfer follow the service: allow earned benefits to move to a spouse or child whenever the family needs it — during service or after — so no one is penalized for separating first or for when their family grows.

Why it matters

Transferability was built as a retention tool, but it has become a trap for career members. Enlisted families disproportionately grow later in a career and lose out. Fairness means the benefit follows the service, not the paperwork deadline.

Legislative avenues

H.R. 4540 · House
Military Family GI Bill Promise Act
Education · Preserve benefit transferability

Bipartisan House bill (Vindman–Mills) allowing eligible members and veterans to transfer earned benefits to a spouse or child at any time — during service or after separation — including dependents added to the family after retirement, and adding a 10-year service pathway to qualify.

How to explain it
  • Lets the transfer happen at any time — while serving or after separation
  • Covers a spouse or child added to the family after retirement
  • Adds a 10-year service pathway to qualify

Senate companion effort
Post-9/11 GI Bill Transferability Entitlement Act
Education · Preserve benefit transferability

Senate companion effort (Blumenthal) ensuring any member who has completed ten years of service can transfer benefits to dependents at any time — on active duty or as a veteran — including those who separated without transferring or gained a dependent after retirement.

  • Ten years of service unlocks the ability to transfer
  • Transfer allowed at any time — on active duty or as a veteran
  • Covers members who separated without transferring or gained a dependent after retirement
